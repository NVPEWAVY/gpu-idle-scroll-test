Post-idle lag test

The page waits for the chosen delay, then scrolls a heavy fragment by itself and measures every frame. The «cold» pass comes right after waking up, followed by a «warm» pass as a control. Run it on an old and a new graphics card at different delays and compare.

Wake
Time from when the test issued the scroll command to the first frame. At 60 Hz the norm is 17–35 ms.
Stable after
After how many ms the frames become steady (15 in a row no longer than 1.5 frames).
Extra time, 600 ms
Sum of all «extra» ms over a normal frame during the first 600 ms. Noticeable from 15 ms, dramatic from 60 ms.
Dropped frames
How many frames were not shown on time (estimated from the interval lengths).
The rest
In the worst frame: how much the browser main thread took (per LoAF) and how much remained outside it — GPU, compositor, driver, vsync. This is an estimate, not a direct measurement, and requires Chrome or Edge.

Report for NVIDIA

To see what the card is doing at the moment of lag. Start this in PowerShell before the test and don't close it until the end:

nvidia-smi --query-gpu=timestamp,pstate,pcie.link.gen.current,clocks.gr,clocks.mem,power.draw,utilization.gpu --format=csv -lms 100 -f idle-log.csv

The «Scroll start» time in the table has the same format as the timestamp in the log. Find that moment in the file and look at pstate, pcie.link.gen.current and clocks: that shows when the card was raising clocks and PCIe.
